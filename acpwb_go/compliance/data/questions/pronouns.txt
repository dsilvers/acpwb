# Names and pronouns (ACPWB respectful workplace policy).
Q: Using the name a colleague asks to be called is:
+ A basic part of ACPWB's respectful workplace policy
- Optional
- Only for new hires
- Only for executives

Q: If you are unsure what pronouns a colleague uses, a respectful approach is to:
+ Use their name, or ask privately and politely if it is relevant
- Guess based on appearance
- Ask in front of the whole team
- Avoid speaking to them

Q: If you accidentally use the wrong name or pronoun, you should:
+ Briefly correct yourself and move on
- Make a long apology that draws attention
- Pretend it did not happen
- Argue about it

Q: Repeatedly and deliberately using a name a colleague has asked you not to use:
+ Can violate ACPWB's respectful workplace and harassment policies
- Is fine if it is a nickname
- Is a joke
- Is required

Q: Adding your pronouns to your email signature is:
+ Optional and never required
- Mandatory
- Prohibited
- Required for managers

Q: A colleague updates their name in the directory. Your team should:
+ Use the updated name going forward
- Keep using the old name
- Ask for documentation
- Vote on it

Q: Asking a colleague about their private medical history because of their gender identity is:
+ Inappropriate and intrusive
- Friendly curiosity
- Required
- A team activity

Q: Mispronouncing a colleague's name repeatedly after being corrected:
+ Is disrespectful; practice and get it right
- Is unavoidable
- Is fine forever
- Is their problem

Q: Names on badges and email accounts can be changed:
+ Through HR and IT processes, even before a legal name change in many systems
- Never
- Only after five years
- Only by the CEO

Q: Gossiping about a colleague's gender identity is:
+ Disrespectful and can violate policy
- Harmless
- Encouraged
- Allowed in private chat

Q: Respect for names and pronouns applies:
+ In meetings, documents, chat, and conversations about the person
- Only face to face
- Only in writing
- Only when the person is present
