# Ideas, hackathons, and innovation programs.

Q: Ideas submitted to the ACPWB innovation program generally:
+ Belong to ACPWB under your invention assignment agreement
- Belong to whoever submits first, personally
- Belong to the judges
- Belong to {VENDOR}

Q: Hackathon projects should use:
+ Test or synthetic data, not real customer or employee data, unless approved
- Production customer data for realism
- Data scraped from competitors
- Coworkers' passwords

Q: Sharing an unreleased ACPWB product idea at a public meetup is:
+ Not allowed without approval; it may harm patent rights and confidentiality
- Good networking
- Required for feedback
- Allowed if you do not use the name

Q: A "lunch and learn" session is:
+ An informal learning session, often over lunch
- A required certification exam
- A cooking class
- A meeting about lunch

Q: Pilot projects using new software must:
+ Go through security and procurement review before using ACPWB data
- Start immediately with a free trial
- Use personal credit cards
- Avoid IT to move faster

Q: The "suggestion box" is meant for:
+ Constructive ideas and concerns
- Lunch orders
- Complaints about named coworkers' appearance
- Resignation letters

Q: Failure in an approved experiment should be:
+ Shared as learning
- Hidden
- Punished
- Blamed on the {COMMITTEE}

Q: Prototypes built at a hackathon should not be:
+ Deployed to production without normal review
- Demonstrated
- Documented
- Celebrated
