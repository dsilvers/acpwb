# Event catering and food handling for company events.
Q: Hot catered food should be held at:
+ 135°F or above
- Room temperature
- 100°F
- Whatever the chafing fuel allows

Q: Cold catered food should be held at:
+ 41°F or below
- 60°F
- Room temperature, if covered
- Whatever the cooler says on the box

Q: Food left out without temperature control should be discarded after:
+ 4 hours, or sooner if the caterer's plan says so
- The end of the week
- Someone notices a smell
- The next all-hands meeting

Q: When ordering for an event, you should ask attendees about:
+ Food allergies and dietary restrictions
- Their salary
- Their favorite catering company's stock price
- Their home address

Q: Catered food labels should identify:
+ Major allergens, such as peanuts, tree nuts, milk, eggs, wheat, soy, fish, shellfish, and sesame
- The caterer's political views
- The calorie count only
- Who ordered the food

Q: Serving utensils at a buffet should:
+ Be provided for each dish so guests do not use their hands
- Be shared between dishes to save space
- Be optional
- Be the guests' own forks

Q: Leftover catered food can be offered to employees when:
+ It has been held at safe temperatures and is within safe time limits
- It has been out all afternoon
- No one can remember when it arrived
- It has a sticky note saying "probably fine"

Q: A caterer offering a free weekend at their lake house to the event planner is:
+ A gift from a vendor that must follow the gift policy
- A thank-you everyone should accept
- Required for good vendor relations
- A form of carbon offset

Q: Alcohol at company events requires:
+ Approval under the events policy, licensed servers, and non-alcoholic options
- A team member mixing drinks
- Nothing; it is a party
- Everyone to finish their drink before leaving

Q: A guest says the cake may have touched peanuts. You should:
+ Label it clearly and tell guests with allergies
- Scrape off the visible peanuts
- Say nothing
- Serve it only to people who look healthy

Q: Catering invoices must be:
+ Matched to the approved order and coded to the right cost center
- Paid in cash from petty cash
- Split into smaller invoices to avoid approval limits
- Approved by the caterer
