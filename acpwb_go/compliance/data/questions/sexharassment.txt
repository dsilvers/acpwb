# Sexual harassment prevention. Answers follow EEOC guidance on Title VII.
Q: Sexual harassment includes:
+ Unwelcome sexual advances, requests for sexual favors, and other verbal or physical conduct of a sexual nature
- Only physical touching
- Only conduct by a supervisor
- Only conduct that happens in the office

Q: Quid pro quo harassment happens when:
+ A job benefit or decision is conditioned on submitting to sexual conduct
- Two employees trade shifts
- A manager gives feedback
- Coworkers exchange gifts

Q: A hostile work environment can be created by conduct that is:
+ Severe or pervasive enough to make the workplace intimidating, hostile, or offensive
- Mildly annoying once
- Any disagreement
- A single unpopular decision

Q: Sexual harassment can be committed by:
+ Supervisors, coworkers, customers, vendors, or other non-employees
- Only supervisors
- Only employees of the opposite sex
- Only people in the same department

Q: The victim and the harasser:
+ Can be of any gender, including the same gender
- Must be of different genders
- Must work in the same building
- Must be the same age

Q: Whether conduct is "unwelcome" depends on:
+ Whether the person subjected to it welcomed it, not whether the harasser intended harm
- The harasser's intentions only
- Whether the person complained immediately
- Whether others laughed

Q: Displaying sexually explicit images at work is:
+ Prohibited and can contribute to a hostile environment
- Fine if it is on your own screen
- Fine if it is a joke
- Allowed after hours

Q: A manager who receives a sexual harassment complaint must:
+ Report it to HR promptly, even if asked to keep it quiet
- Handle it privately without HR
- Wait for a second complaint
- Ask the employees to work it out

Q: Repeated requests for a date after a coworker has said no:
+ Can be sexual harassment
- Show persistence
- Are a private matter
- Are allowed outside work hours

Q: Sexual harassment protections apply:
+ At work, at off-site events, during travel, and in online communications
- Only in the office
- Only during business hours
- Only to full-time employees

Q: An employee who reports sexual harassment in good faith is:
+ Protected from retaliation
- Required to prove it first
- Moved to another team automatically
- Responsible for investigating

Q: Some states, such as California and New York, require:
+ Regular sexual harassment prevention training for employees
- No training
- Training only for executives
- Training once in a lifetime
