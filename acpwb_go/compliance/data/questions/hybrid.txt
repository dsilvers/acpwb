# Hybrid work: schedules, expectations, and rules.

Q: [Anchor days|Team anchor days] in a hybrid schedule are:

- Days when no one may come in
- Days reserved for executives
- Days on which meetings are prohibited

+ Agreed with your manager, recorded in your hybrid agreement, and kept consistently
- Chosen each morning based on the weather

+ Record all hours worked, including time spent checking email after hours
- Only record time spent in the office
- Work unpaid overtime at home
- Round their hours to the nearest day

+ Make sure remote attendees can see, hear, and contribute, for example by using a room camera and calling on them
- Let in-room attendees speak first and remote attendees only if time permits
- Turn off the remote audio to reduce noise
- Hold side conversations in the room

+ Requires approval in advance, because of tax, immigration, data, and employment law
- Is allowed if you stay on your normal hours
- Is allowed if you use a VPN
- Requires only an out-of-office message

Q: [Hybrid associates|Associates on a hybrid schedule] are expected to be reachable during:
+ Their agreed working hours, wherever they are working
- Any time, day or night
- Only when in the office
- Only between {TIMEOFDAY} and lunch

Q: [Hybrid schedule exceptions|Temporary exceptions to your hybrid schedule], such as for a family need, should be:
+ Requested from your manager and documented

- Covered by having a colleague badge in for you
- Avoided at all costs

Q: [Equipment|Your equipment] for hybrid work, such as a laptop and headset, should:
+ Travel with you between home and office and be kept secure in transit
- Be left in the car overnight
- Be duplicated at your own expense
- Be stored at the office on remote days
