# Copyright basics for employees (U.S. law in general terms).
Q: Copyright protects:
+ Original works of authorship, such as text, images, software, and music
- Ideas and facts themselves
- Company names
- Inventions

Q: A photo found through a web search can be used in a company presentation:
+ Only if we have a license or permission, or the use is clearly allowed
- Freely, because it was on the internet
- If you crop out the watermark
- If you give it a new file name

Q: Removing a watermark from a stock photo to avoid paying for it is:
+ Copyright infringement and prohibited
- Clever budgeting
- Allowed for internal slides
- Required by the brand team

Q: Work that employees create as part of their job is generally owned by:
+ The employer, under the work-for-hire rules and their agreements
- The employee's family
- The internet
- Whoever saves the file first

Q: Fair use is:
+ A limited legal defense judged case by case, not a blanket permission
- Permission to copy anything for business use
- A rule that allows 30 seconds of any song
- A type of software license

Q: Sharing a paid newsletter or research report with the whole company without a license is:
+ Likely a violation of the publisher's terms and copyright
- Fine because we paid once
- Encouraged to save money
- Allowed if you rename the file

Q: Playing music at a company event open to the public may require:
+ A performance license, which the venue or Events team should confirm
- Nothing; music is free at events
- Asking the band's fans
- Turning the volume down

Q: Software must be used:
+ In line with its license terms, including the number of users
- On as many machines as you like
- Only after removing the license key
- Without reading the license

Q: Quoting a short passage from a book with attribution in an internal report:
+ May be fine use, but substantial copying needs permission
- Is always theft
- Requires paying the author per word
- Transfers the copyright to ACPWB

Q: Copyright protection starts:
+ As soon as an original work is fixed in a tangible form
- Only after registration with the government
- After 10 years
- Only when a lawyer is involved

Q: If you are unsure whether you can use someone else's content, ask:
+ Legal or the brand team before using it
- The person sitting next to you
- A search engine
- Nobody; just use it
