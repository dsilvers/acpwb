# Inventory control.
Q: A cycle count is:
+ Counting a portion of inventory on a regular schedule instead of all at once
- Counting bicycles in the parking lot
- Counting the same item over and over
- An annual party

Q: Inventory adjustments to match a count should be:
+ Documented, investigated if large, and approved
- Made quietly to hide shrink
- Made by whoever is counting
- Avoided by recounting until it matches

Q: Shrink refers to:
+ Inventory loss from theft, damage, or errors
- Smaller packaging
- A warehouse that is getting smaller
- Washing uniforms

Q: The person who receives inventory should not also:
+ Approve payment for it and adjust inventory records without oversight
- Sign the delivery receipt
- Check the packing slip
- Count the boxes

Q: Damaged inventory should be:
+ Recorded and handled under the damaged goods procedure
- Hidden behind good stock
- Taken home
- Sold for cash

Q: Taking company inventory for personal use is:
+ Theft and prohibited
- A perk
- Allowed if it is going to be discarded
- Allowed for small items

Q: Blind counts, where the counter does not see the system quantity, help to:
+ Prevent counters from simply confirming the expected number
- Count faster in the dark
- Hide inventory
- Save printer paper

Q: Obsolete inventory should be:
+ Identified, reviewed, and written off or disposed of with approval
- Kept forever
- Moved to someone's garage
- Recounted every day

Q: Controlled or high-value items should be stored:
+ In secured locations with limited access and frequent counts
- Near the exit for easy shipping
- Unlabeled
- In the break room

Q: A shipment that arrives without a matching purchase order should be:
+ Held and reported to Purchasing for review
- Accepted and used
- Returned without telling anyone
- Opened and kept

Q: First-in, first-out (FIFO) rotation helps:
+ Use older stock before it expires or becomes obsolete
- Fill the warehouse faster
- Hide damaged goods
- Speed up counts
