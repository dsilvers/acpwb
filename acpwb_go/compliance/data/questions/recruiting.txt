# Fair and lawful recruiting.
Q: Job postings should describe:
+ The essential duties and qualifications for the role
- The ideal candidate's age
- The preferred gender
- The manager's hobbies

Q: Many states and cities prohibit employers from asking applicants about:
+ Their salary history
- Their work experience
- Their skills
- Their availability

Q: Several states require job postings to include:
+ A good-faith pay range
- The CEO's salary
- The previous person's salary
- No pay information

Q: Structured interviews, where each candidate is asked the same job-related questions, help to:
+ Reduce bias and make fair comparisons
- Make interviews longer
- Find personal information
- Replace the job description

Q: Asking a candidate whether they plan to have children is:
+ Improper and can lead to discrimination claims
- A good culture-fit question
- Required for scheduling
- Friendly small talk

Q: Background checks through a consumer reporting agency require:
+ A standalone written disclosure and the candidate's authorization under the FCRA
- No consent
- The candidate's social media passwords
- A handshake

Q: Recruiters should keep interview notes:
+ Factual, job-related, and stored under the records policy
- Personal and humorous
- On sticky notes thrown away later
- On social media

Q: Rejecting a candidate because of an accent that does not affect job performance can be:
+ National origin discrimination
- A business necessity
- Required
- A best practice

Q: Hiring a relative of the hiring manager:
+ Must follow the nepotism and conflict of interest policies
- Is always encouraged
- Never needs disclosure
- Speeds up onboarding

Q: Reasonable accommodations in the hiring process, such as extra time for a test, are:
+ Required under the ADA when needed and requested
- Optional favors
- Not allowed
- Only for internal candidates

Q: Candidate personal data should be:
+ Used only for recruiting and protected under privacy policies
- Shared with marketing
- Posted on the team board
- Sold to other recruiters
