# Mentoring programs.
Q: A mentor's role is to:
+ Share experience and guidance to support the mentee's growth
- Do the mentee's work
- Evaluate their pay
- Approve their expenses

Q: Mentoring conversations are:
+ Confidential, except where safety or policy violations require reporting
- Public
- Recorded
- Shared with managers

Q: A mentor who learns of harassment from a mentee should:
+ Encourage reporting and follow the policy's reporting obligations
- Keep it secret always
- Ignore it
- Confront the harasser

Q: Mentoring relationships should be:
+ Professional and free from romantic involvement
- Romantic
- Secret
- Unstructured always

Q: Good mentees:
+ Come prepared with goals and questions
- Expect the mentor to drive everything
- Skip meetings
- Ask for promotions

Q: Reverse mentoring means:
+ Junior employees mentoring senior leaders on topics they know well
- Mentoring backwards in time
- Ending mentoring
- Avoiding mentoring

Q: Mentoring programs support inclusion when:
+ They are open and accessible to everyone
- Only favorites join
- They are secret
- They exclude new hires

Q: Mentors should avoid:
+ Promising outcomes such as promotions
- Giving advice
- Sharing experiences
- Listening

Q: Mentoring time is:
+ Part of work for program participants
- Unpaid volunteer time always
- Prohibited
- Vacation

Q: Ending a mentoring relationship should be:
+ Done respectfully when goals are met or it is not working
- Done by ghosting
- Prohibited
- Punished
