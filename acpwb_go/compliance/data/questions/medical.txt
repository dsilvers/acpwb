# Medical emergencies: allergy, choking, stroke, and overdose. Correct
# answers follow Red Cross, AHA, and CDC/FDA guidance; keep them accurate.

Q: Signs of anaphylaxis include:
+ Trouble breathing, swelling of the face or throat, hives, and dizziness after exposure to an allergen
- A mild sneeze
- An itchy elbow only
- Craving dessert

Q: For someone having anaphylaxis who has an epinephrine auto-injector, you should:
+ Help them use it in the outer thigh, even through clothing, and call 911
- Wait to see if it passes
- Give them water and wait
- Inject it into their arm only after removing their shirt

Q: After using an epinephrine auto-injector, the person should still:
+ Get emergency medical care, since symptoms can return
- Return to work immediately
- Avoid telling anyone
- Use a second injector right away no matter what

Q: The universal sign for choking is:
+ Hands clutched to the throat
- A thumbs up
- Waving both arms
- Pointing at the ceiling

Q: An adult who is choking but can cough forcefully should be:
+ Encouraged to keep coughing
- Given water immediately
- Given abdominal thrusts right away
- Slapped hard on the back while lying down

Q: An adult who is choking and cannot speak, cough, or breathe needs:
+ Abdominal thrusts (and back blows, per your training) and a call to 911
- A glass of water
- Time to recover on their own
- A walk outside

Q: F.A.S.T. stands for the warning signs of stroke:
+ Face drooping, Arm weakness, Speech difficulty, Time to call 911
- Fever, Ache, Sneeze, Tired
- Food, Alcohol, Sleep, Travel
- Fast, Accurate, Secure, Timely

Q: When you suspect a stroke, it is important to note:
+ The time symptoms started
- The person's favorite color
- The weather
- The nearest restaurant

Q: Signs of an opioid overdose include:
+ Unresponsiveness, slow or stopped breathing, pinpoint pupils, and blue or gray lips
- Laughing loudly
- Talking quickly
- Sweating from exercise

Q: Naloxone (for example, Narcan nasal spray) is:
+ A medicine that can reverse an opioid overdose, available without a prescription
- A pain reliever
- A sleeping aid
- A type of energy drink

Q: After giving naloxone, you should:
+ Call 911, stay with the person, and give another dose if they do not respond in 2 to 3 minutes
- Leave them to sleep it off
- Give them coffee
- Put them in a cold shower

Q: Many states have Good Samaritan laws that:
+ Offer some legal protection to people who call 911 to help someone who is overdosing
- Require you to pay for the ambulance
- Penalize people who help
- Apply only to doctors
