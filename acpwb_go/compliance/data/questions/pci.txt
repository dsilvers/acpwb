# Payment card data. Answers follow PCI DSS in general terms.
Q: PCI DSS is:
+ A security standard for organizations that store, process, or transmit payment card data
- A tax form
- A type of credit card
- A vacation policy

Q: After a card payment is authorized, the card verification code (CVV) may be stored:
+ Never
- Forever
- For 30 days
- Only on paper

Q: Taking card numbers by email or chat is:
+ Not allowed
- Fine if the customer offers
- Required for speed
- Allowed with a password

Q: When showing a card number on a screen or receipt, at most:
+ The first six and last four digits may be shown
- All 16 digits
- The middle eight digits
- The CVV

Q: Card data written on paper during a phone order must be:
+ Avoided; if it happens, secured and destroyed promptly by cross-cut shredding
- Kept in the desk
- Taped to the monitor
- Filed with the order forever

Q: Payment terminals should be inspected for:
+ Tampering or skimming devices
- Dust only
- Software ads
- Color fading

Q: Systems that handle card data should be:
+ Segmented from other networks and limited to authorized staff
- On the guest Wi-Fi
- Accessible to everyone
- Unmonitored

Q: Recording calls while customers read card numbers aloud:
+ Must be paused or the recording must be protected under PCI rules
- Is fine and should be shared
- Is required
- Improves training

Q: A customer emails their card number to you anyway. You should:
+ Not process it from email, delete it securely as instructed, and follow the procedure
- Process it and reply
- Forward it to billing
- Save it for next time

Q: Default passwords on payment systems:
+ Must be changed
- Are fine
- Are required by the vendor
- Should be posted

Q: A suspected card data breach should be reported:
+ Immediately to Security
- At month end
- Only if customers complain
- To the card brands by the employee directly
