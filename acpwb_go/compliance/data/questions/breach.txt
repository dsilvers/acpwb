# Personal data breach response. Notification rules follow GDPR Arts. 33–34
# and U.S. state breach laws in general terms.
Q: If you suspect personal data has been exposed, your first step is:
+ Report it to the Security or Privacy team immediately
- Investigate quietly for a few weeks
- Delete the evidence
- Post about it on social media

Q: Under GDPR, a controller must normally notify the supervisory authority of a reportable breach:
+ Within 72 hours of becoming aware of it
- Within 72 business days
- Only if the press finds out
- At the next annual audit

Q: Under GDPR, affected individuals must be told about a breach when it is likely to result in:
+ A high risk to their rights and freedoms
- Any inconvenience at all
- A delay in shipping
- A change in the privacy policy

Q: Which U.S. states have data breach notification laws?
+ All 50 states
- Only California
- Only states with ocean coastline
- None; it is purely federal

Q: A laptop holding customer data is stolen, but its disk is strongly encrypted and the key is safe. This is:
+ Still reported internally, and often not a notifiable breach because the data is unreadable
- Ignored because laptops are cheap
- Automatically notifiable to every customer worldwide
- Reported only if the laptop was new

Q: During a breach investigation, you should:
+ Preserve logs and evidence and follow the incident response team's direction
- Wipe the affected systems right away
- Contact the attacker to negotiate
- Tell customers yourself before Legal reviews the facts

Q: Sending a spreadsheet of employee Social Security numbers to the wrong external email address is:
+ A potential data breach that must be reported right away
- Fine if you send a follow-up saying "please delete"
- Only a problem if the recipient complains
- A routine email error

Q: Who decides whether regulators and individuals must be notified?
+ The Privacy, Legal, and Security teams, following the response plan
- Whoever discovered the breach
- The vendor involved
- The first customer who asks

Q: Breach records, including breaches that were not notifiable, should be:
+ Documented with the facts, effects, and actions taken
- Shredded after the meeting
- Kept only in someone's memory
- Posted in the break room

Q: A vendor that processes ACPWB personal data has a breach. The vendor should:
+ Notify ACPWB promptly, as required by our contract
- Keep it quiet to protect the relationship
- Notify only its own customers
- Wait until contract renewal

Q: Calling a breach "minor" in an email before the investigation is done is:
+ Unwise; describe facts, not conclusions, until the team has assessed it
- Required to keep morale up
- A best practice for legal privilege
- The only allowed subject line
