# Video meetings.

Q: When you are not speaking in a large video meeting, your microphone should usually be:
+ Muted
- Unmuted, so others can hear your typing and know you are working
- Turned up to the maximum
- Pointed at the dishwasher

Q: You begin speaking and notice no one reacts. The most likely cause is:
+ You are muted
- Everyone agrees with you completely
- The video platform is down for maintenance
- The meeting is a recording

Q: Recording a video meeting should be done:
+ Only with notice to participants and consent where the law or policy requires it
- Secretly, for accuracy
- Always, by every participant
- Only if the meeting is boring

Q: Before sharing your screen, you should:
+ Close unrelated tabs, messages, and documents that others should not see
- Open your personal email for reference
- Maximize your notifications
- Display your password manager for transparency

Q: A whiteboard behind you shows a confidential {DOCUMENT}. You should:
+ Erase it, move it out of view, or use a background blur
- Leave it, since no one reads whiteboards
- Point to it
- Zoom in on it for the people in the back

Q: Joining a video meeting while driving is:
+ Unsafe; join by audio only if you must, or reschedule
- Fine with your camera on
- Fine if you present slides
- Required to show commitment

Q: For a meeting that could have been an email, the meeting organizer should:
+ Send an email
- Schedule a longer meeting to discuss it
- Add a pre-meeting to plan the meeting
- Invite the {COMMITTEE}

Q: Your camera is on and you need to step away for a moment. You should:
+ Turn your camera off and mute
- Leave your chair in view to show you are coming back
- Place a photo of yourself in front of the camera
- Leave the meeting without telling anyone, forever
