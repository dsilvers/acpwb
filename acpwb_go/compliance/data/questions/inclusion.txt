# Inclusion and belonging at work.
Q: An inclusive workplace is one where:
+ Everyone is treated with respect and has a fair chance to contribute and advance
- Everyone has the same opinions
- Only the majority's preferences count
- No one disagrees

Q: Employee resource groups (ERGs) at ACPWB are:
+ Voluntary groups open to all employees
- Required for certain employees
- Closed to allies
- Secret societies

Q: Using a coworker's correct name and its pronunciation is:
+ A basic sign of respect
- Optional
- Only for executives
- A matter of preference for the speaker

Q: In meetings, inclusive behavior includes:
+ Making room for quieter voices and crediting ideas to the people who offered them
- Interrupting to keep momentum
- Letting the loudest person decide
- Repeating others' ideas as your own

Q: Scheduling team events only at bars after hours can:
+ Exclude people who do not drink or have caregiving duties
- Include everyone
- Improve accessibility
- Satisfy every religious calendar

Q: A colleague says your comment was hurtful. A good first response is:
+ Listen, thank them, and apologize for the impact
- Explain why they are wrong
- Tell them it was a joke
- Report them for being sensitive

Q: Holidays and observances that are not on the company calendar:
+ Can be accommodated through floating holidays or PTO, and respected in scheduling
- Must be ignored
- Are banned from discussion
- Are unpaid by law

Q: Accessibility in documents includes:
+ Alt text for images, good color contrast, and readable formatting
- Using red text on green backgrounds
- Images of text only
- Tiny fonts to fit more content

Q: Inclusion training asks employees to:
+ Treat colleagues with respect and follow ACPWB's policies, regardless of personal beliefs
- Agree with every opinion in the training
- Share their private beliefs
- Change their religion

Q: A job posting with phrases like "young and energetic team" can:
+ Discourage older applicants and raise age discrimination concerns
- Attract the best candidates
- Be required for startups
- Improve diversity

Q: Mentoring and sponsorship programs help inclusion by:
+ Giving people access to guidance and opportunities they might not otherwise get
- Giving extra work to mentors only
- Replacing performance reviews
- Excluding new employees
