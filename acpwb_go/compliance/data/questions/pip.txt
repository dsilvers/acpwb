# Performance improvement plans.
Q: A performance improvement plan (PIP) should include:
+ Specific goals, support, check-ins, and a timeline
- Vague expectations
- No timeline
- Secret goals

Q: The purpose of a PIP is to:
+ Give the employee a clear opportunity to improve
- Build a case only
- Punish
- Embarrass

Q: PIP goals should be:
+ Measurable and achievable within the time given
- Impossible
- Changed weekly without notice
- Secret

Q: During a PIP, managers should:
+ Meet regularly and give honest feedback
- Avoid the employee
- Assign extra impossible work
- Wait until the end

Q: Placing someone on a PIP right after they complain about discrimination:
+ May look retaliatory and needs HR and Legal review
- Is normal
- Is required
- Is smart

Q: A PIP should be documented:
+ In writing, with the employee receiving a copy
- Verbally only
- Not at all
- On social media

Q: Employees on a PIP can:
+ Ask questions and request resources
- Not ask anything
- Not talk to HR
- Not respond

Q: Successful completion of a PIP means:
+ Expectations were met and normal performance management continues
- Termination
- Demotion
- Nothing

Q: A PIP for an employee on protected leave should be:
+ Reviewed with HR to avoid interfering with leave rights
- Started during leave
- Hidden
- Ignored

Q: PIP information is:
+ Confidential
- Public
- Shared with the team
- Posted
