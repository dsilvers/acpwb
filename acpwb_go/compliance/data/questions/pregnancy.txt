# Pregnancy at work. Answers follow the Pregnancy Discrimination Act,
# the Pregnant Workers Fairness Act, and the PUMP for Nursing Mothers Act.
Q: The Pregnancy Discrimination Act makes it unlawful to:
+ Treat an employee or applicant unfavorably because of pregnancy, childbirth, or related conditions
- Ask about due dates for party planning
- Offer parental leave
- Provide accommodations

Q: The Pregnant Workers Fairness Act requires employers with 15 or more employees to:
+ Provide reasonable accommodations for known limitations related to pregnancy, childbirth, or related conditions unless there is undue hardship
- Pay a pregnancy bonus
- Give 12 months off
- Only offer unpaid leave

Q: Common pregnancy accommodations include:
+ More frequent breaks, a stool to sit on, temporary changes to lifting duties, and time off for appointments
- Demotion
- Reduced pay
- Mandatory leave

Q: Under the PWFA, an employer generally may not:
+ Force an employee to take leave if another accommodation would let them keep working
- Offer accommodations
- Talk with the employee
- Provide breaks

Q: The PUMP Act requires most employers to provide nursing employees:
+ Reasonable break time and a private space that is not a bathroom to express breast milk for one year after the child's birth
- A bathroom stall
- No breaks
- Breaks only for executives

Q: Refusing to hire a qualified applicant because she is pregnant is:
+ Unlawful discrimination
- A business decision
- Allowed during busy seasons
- Required for safety

Q: A manager tells a pregnant employee she cannot travel for work "for her own good," even though she wants to and can. This is:
+ Likely pregnancy discrimination
- Protective and required
- Good management
- Required by law

Q: Pregnancy-related limitations covered by the PWFA can include:
+ Morning sickness, lifting restrictions, and recovery from childbirth
- Only complications requiring hospitalization
- Only limitations that last a year
- Nothing temporary

Q: Comments that a pregnant employee will be "less committed" are:
+ Discriminatory stereotypes
- Fair predictions
- Required documentation
- Performance feedback

Q: Accommodation requests related to pregnancy should be handled:
+ Promptly through HR's interactive process
- After the baby is born
- By coworkers
- Never

Q: Retaliating against an employee for requesting a pregnancy accommodation is:
+ Prohibited
- Allowed
- Required
- A best practice
