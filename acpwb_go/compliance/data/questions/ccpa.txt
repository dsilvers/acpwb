# California Consumer Privacy Act (as amended by CPRA), in general terms.
Q: The CCPA gives California residents the right to:
+ Know, delete, and correct personal information, and opt out of its sale or sharing
- Receive free company stock
- Edit the company website
- Veto product launches

Q: Since January 1, 2023, the CCPA's rights also cover:
+ Employees and job applicants who are California residents
- Only customers who spend more than $100
- Only residents of Los Angeles
- Nobody new

Q: A business must generally respond to a verifiable consumer request within:
+ 45 days, which can be extended once by another 45 days with notice
- 24 hours
- One year
- Whenever the privacy team gets to it

Q: A website covered by the CCPA that sells or shares personal information must offer:
+ A clear "Do Not Sell or Share My Personal Information" link or equivalent opt-out
- A pop-up that cannot be closed
- A fax number for opt-outs
- A puzzle game to opt out

Q: Under California's rules, a business must treat a browser Global Privacy Control signal as:
+ A valid request to opt out of sale or sharing
- Spam
- An optional suggestion
- A request to delete the account

Q: Charging a higher price to a consumer because they exercised a CCPA right is:
+ Prohibited discrimination under the law, except for permitted financial incentives
- Encouraged
- Required
- Allowed on weekends

Q: "Sensitive personal information" under CPRA includes:
+ Social Security numbers, precise geolocation, and account login credentials
- Favorite colors
- Job titles
- Public press releases

Q: If a customer calls asking what personal data ACPWB holds about them, you should:
+ Route the request to the Privacy team's request process
- Read their file to them over the phone right away
- Tell them we have no data
- Ask them to submit the request by carrier pigeon

Q: The agency that enforces California privacy law along with the Attorney General is:
+ The California Privacy Protection Agency
- The California Department of Snacks
- The Federal Aviation Administration
- The Milwaukee County Clerk

Q: Personal information collected for one purpose should be used for:
+ Purposes consistent with what consumers were told when it was collected
- Any purpose the marketing team invents later
- Resale without notice
- Training games

Q: Before a new vendor receives California consumer data, ACPWB should:
+ Have a contract that limits the vendor's use of the data
- Send the data first and sign later
- Assume the vendor is trustworthy
- Ask the vendor to pinky-promise
