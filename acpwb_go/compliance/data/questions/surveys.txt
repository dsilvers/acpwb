# Employee surveys and research ethics.
Q: Responses to the confidential engagement survey are:
+ Reported in groups large enough to protect individual identity
- Shown to managers by name
- Posted publicly
- Used for discipline

Q: Managers trying to identify who wrote a survey comment is:
+ Inappropriate and undermines trust
- Encouraged
- Required
- Fun

Q: Survey results are most useful when:
+ Leaders share them and act on them
- They are kept secret
- They are deleted
- They are only collected

Q: Pressuring employees to give high scores is:
+ Not allowed
- Good for metrics
- Required
- A morale booster

Q: Customer surveys must:
+ Follow privacy rules and disclose how responses are used
- Collect as much data as possible
- Be mandatory
- Hide the sponsor

Q: A survey of 4 people reported as "100% positive" may:
+ Be misleading because the sample is too small
- Be the best result
- Prove everything
- Be required

Q: Survey questions should be:
+ Clear, neutral, and not leading
- Designed to get the answer leadership wants
- Long and confusing
- Double-barreled

Q: Offering incentives for survey participation:
+ Is fine if it does not pressure answers or break rules
- Is illegal
- Must depend on giving high scores
- Is required

Q: Raw survey data with identifiable responses should be:
+ Restricted to those who need it and protected
- Shared widely
- Printed for the team
- Emailed to all

Q: A survey asking about health conditions requires:
+ Careful privacy and legal review
- Nothing special
- Names on every response
- Manager review of each answer

Q: The ACPWB research reports that publish survey data must:
+ Describe methods and sample sizes honestly
- Hide methods
- Invent respondents
- Round to make results look better
