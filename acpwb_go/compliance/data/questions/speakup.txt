# Speaking up and raising concerns.
Q: Ways to raise a concern at ACPWB include:
+ Your manager, HR, Legal, Ethics, or the anonymous Ethics Hotline
- Only the CEO
- Only social media
- Only after you leave the company

Q: Concerns raised in good faith are:
+ Protected from retaliation
- Discouraged
- Grounds for discipline if not proven
- Ignored

Q: "Good faith" means:
+ You honestly believe the concern is true, even if it turns out you were wrong
- You can prove everything
- You have a lawyer
- You raise it nicely

Q: Anonymous reports are:
+ Accepted and investigated, though more detail helps the investigation
- Ignored
- Illegal
- Treated as gossip

Q: Investigations into concerns are:
+ Kept as confidential as possible
- Announced company-wide
- Posted on the intranet
- Shared with the person accused immediately in full

Q: Managers who receive a concern should:
+ Listen, thank the person, and escalate it under the policy
- Decide alone that it is not serious
- Tell the person to stop complaining
- Share it with the team

Q: If you are asked to do something you think is illegal, you should:
+ Refuse and raise the concern
- Do it and hope for the best
- Do half of it
- Ask a coworker to do it instead

Q: Speaking up early about a problem:
+ Usually makes it easier and cheaper to fix
- Causes trouble for everyone
- Is not allowed
- Is a sign of weakness

Q: Company policies do not prevent employees from:
+ Reporting possible violations of law to government agencies
- Following policies
- Raising concerns internally
- Asking questions

Q: The Ethics Hotline is operated by:
+ An independent provider so reports can be made anonymously
- The person you report
- Your manager
- Marketing

Q: After you raise a concern, you can expect:
+ Follow-up when appropriate, though some details may stay confidential
- Every detail of any discipline
- No response ever
- A reward
