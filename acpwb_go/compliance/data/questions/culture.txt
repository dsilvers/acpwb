# Meetings, communication, and everyday office culture at ACPWB.

Q: A meeting invitation should include:
+ A clear purpose and agenda, so attendees can prepare or decline
- Only the title "Sync"
- A recurring series with no end date
- Everyone in the department, just in case

Q: Replying all to a company-wide email to say "Please remove me" is:
+ Discouraged; reply only to the sender or the list owner
- The fastest way to be removed
- Required for compliance
- A great way to meet colleagues

Q: When you decline a meeting, a courteous step is to:
+ Let the organizer know, and send input or a delegate if needed
- Decline without explanation five minutes before
- Accept and not attend
- Attend and work on email

Q: Sending an email in all capital letters is:
+ Read as shouting
- Required for urgent matters
- Easier to read
- Preferred by {SYSTEM}

Q: A "no meeting day" is meant to:
+ Protect time for focused work
- Be filled with informal meetings
- Allow meetings longer than 2 hours
- Cancel {EVENT}

Q: The person who scheduled a meeting should:
+ Start and end on time and share notes or decisions afterward
- Arrive late to make an entrance
- Extend the meeting if there is time left
- Make it recurring forever

Q: Eating loud snacks while unmuted in a meeting is:
+ Distracting; mute or wait until after
- A sign of comfort
- Required for afternoon meetings
- Encouraged on {WEEKDAY}s

Q: A good status update is:
+ Brief, specific, and highlights blockers
- A 40-slide deck
- A list of everything you did since {YEAR}
- An apology for having no update

Q: "Per my last email" is generally understood as:
+ A pointed reminder; consider a friendlier phrasing
- A warm greeting
- A legal requirement
- A sign-off
