# Employee referral program.
Q: A referral bonus is paid when:
+ The referred candidate is hired and meets the program's conditions
- You send a resume
- The candidate gets an interview
- You refer yourself

Q: Referred candidates go through:
+ The same hiring process and evaluation as other candidates
- A shortcut with no interviews
- The CEO's personal approval
- A separate lottery

Q: Referring a close family member requires:
+ Disclosure under the conflict of interest and nepotism rules
- Nothing
- A larger bonus
- A secret handshake

Q: Hiring managers are generally not eligible for referral bonuses for:
+ Positions they are hiring for
- Any position ever
- Positions in other cities
- Positions they do not know about

Q: Sharing confidential job details or internal salary data with a referral is:
+ Not allowed
- Helpful
- Required
- Encouraged

Q: Pressuring the recruiter to hire your referral is:
+ Inappropriate
- Required
- A good way to win
- Part of the program

Q: Referral programs can support diversity when:
+ Employees refer from broad networks, alongside other sourcing
- They are the only hiring source
- They favor people like current staff
- They exclude new employees

Q: Splitting a referral bonus with the candidate is:
+ Not allowed under program rules
- Encouraged
- Required
- Tax-free

Q: You may refer a former colleague from a competitor as long as:
+ You do not share or ask for the competitor's confidential information
- They bring their customer list
- They copy their files first
- They keep working there secretly

Q: A referral that you do not know well should be described:
+ Honestly, with how you know them
- As your best friend
- As a genius
- As a family member

Q: Questions about referral bonus eligibility go to:
+ HR or the Talent Acquisition team
- The candidate
- Your neighbor
- The bonus calculator
