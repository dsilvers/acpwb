# Gray areas: ethics scenarios that do not fit neatly anywhere else.

Q: Your manager asks you to do something you believe is unethical, but not clearly illegal. You should:
+ Raise your concern, ask questions, and use Speak Up channels if needed
- Do it, since your manager is responsible
- Do it slowly
- Quit without saying why

Q: A useful ethics test before acting is:
+ Would I be comfortable if this appeared in the news or in front of my family?
- Will anyone find out?
- Is it under {AMOUNT}?
- Did someone else do it first?

Q: A vendor accidentally emails you a competitor's bid. You should:
+ Not read or use it, delete it, and notify Legal or Procurement
- Use it to negotiate
- Forward it to your team
- Reply asking for more

Q: You realize a project you led contains a significant error after the report was published. You should:
+ Tell your manager and help correct it
- Wait to see if anyone notices
- Blame the {COMMITTEE}
- Delete the report

Q: A coworker regularly leaves early but asks you to tell people they are in a meeting. You should:
+ Decline to cover for them
- Agree, to be a team player
- Ask for a share of their bonus
- Post about it

Q: A customer overpays an invoice by mistake. ACPWB should:
+ Notify the customer and return or credit the overpayment
- Keep it quietly
- Spend it on {EVENT}
- Wait for them to notice

Q: You overhear two executives discussing an unannounced acquisition in the elevator. You should:
+ Keep it confidential and not trade on it
- Buy the target's stock
- Tell your friends
- Ask them for details

Q: A friend asks you to recommend them for a job you know they are not qualified for. You should:
+ Be honest; you can refer them without overstating their qualifications
- Recommend them strongly
- Edit their résumé
- Hire them yourself

Q: A colleague's expense report includes a dinner you know was personal. You should:
+ Raise it with them or report it through the proper channel
- Ignore it
- Ask them to expense your dinner too
- Approve it to be nice

Q: When unsure whether something is allowed, the best first step is to:
+ Ask: your manager, Compliance, Legal, or HR
- Assume yes
- Assume no and do it anyway
- Ask a chatbot and act on its answer without checking

Q: An "ethical fade" happens when:
+ People stop seeing the ethical side of a decision, framing it only as a business or legal issue
- A poster about ethics loses its color
- Ethics training expires
- Lights dim in the {PLACE}

Q: Small rule-breaking that becomes normal over time is called:
+ Normalization of deviance
- Agile adaptation
- Operational excellence
- Culture
