# Security incident response (NIST SP 800-61 phases in general terms).
Q: The phases of incident response commonly include:
+ Preparation; detection and analysis; containment, eradication, and recovery; and post-incident activity
- Panic, blame, cover-up, and vacation
- Denial, anger, bargaining, and acceptance
- Plan, do, check, act, then delete

Q: The first thing an employee should do when they suspect a security incident is:
+ Report it immediately through the security reporting channel
- Try to fix it themselves
- Turn off every computer in the building
- Wait to see if it gets worse

Q: Containment during an incident aims to:
+ Limit the damage and stop the incident from spreading
- Keep the incident secret from management
- Contain the press release
- Delete all logs

Q: Logs and evidence during an incident should be:
+ Preserved; avoid wiping or rebuilding systems until the response team says so
- Deleted to save space
- Edited to look better
- Shared publicly

Q: An incident commander's role is to:
+ Coordinate the response and make decisions
- Write all the code fixes alone
- Talk to the press without approval
- Assign blame

Q: A "lessons learned" review after an incident should be:
+ Blameless, focused on improving processes and controls
- A search for someone to fire
- Skipped if the incident was small
- Held only if the press noticed

Q: If ransomware appears on your screen, you should:
+ Disconnect from the network if instructed, leave the device on, and report it immediately
- Pay the ransom with a corporate card
- Restart the computer repeatedly
- Email the attackers

Q: Communication with outside parties about an incident should be handled by:
+ Designated people in Legal, Communications, and Security
- Whoever discovered the incident
- The attacker
- The vendor's sales rep

Q: Incident response plans should be:
+ Tested through exercises such as tabletop simulations
- Written and never read
- Kept secret from the response team
- Updated only after a breach

Q: An incident severity level helps the team:
+ Prioritize response and decide who to involve
- Choose the font for the report
- Decide on lunch
- Avoid escalation

Q: Using personal messaging apps to coordinate an incident response:
+ Should be avoided unless they are approved, since records must be kept
- Is required
- Is more secure than company tools
- Is the only option
