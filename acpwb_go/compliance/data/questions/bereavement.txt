# Bereavement leave (ACPWB policy).
Q: Bereavement leave allows employees to:
+ Take time off after the death of a family member or loved one
- Take extra vacation
- Skip training
- Work from the beach

Q: Requests for bereavement leave should go to:
+ Your manager, who records it in the time-off system
- Your coworkers
- Social media
- The vendor

Q: Managers responding to a bereavement request should:
+ Be supportive and approve leave under the policy
- Ask for a death certificate before anything else
- Delay the request
- Ask for details of the death

Q: Additional time beyond bereavement leave can usually come from:
+ PTO or other leave, with manager approval
- Nowhere
- Overtime
- Unpaid fines

Q: Some states:
+ Require employers to provide bereavement leave
- Prohibit bereavement leave
- Limit it to one hour
- Tax it

Q: Coworkers can support a grieving colleague by:
+ Offering condolences and respecting their privacy
- Asking for details
- Gossiping
- Ignoring them completely

Q: The Employee Assistance Program can provide:
+ Confidential grief counseling
- Funeral catering
- Legal judgments
- Nothing

Q: Bereavement leave for the loss of a pregnancy is:
+ Covered under ACPWB's policy
- Not allowed
- Only for one parent
- Unpaid always

Q: Workloads for an employee returning from bereavement leave should be:
+ Discussed with flexibility where possible
- Doubled
- Ignored
- Reassigned permanently

Q: Bereavement information shared with the manager should be:
+ Kept confidential unless the employee wants it shared
- Announced
- Posted
- Shared in a team chat
