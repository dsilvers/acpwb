# Timekeeping for non-exempt employees (FLSA in general terms).
Q: Non-exempt employees must record:
+ All hours actually worked
- Only scheduled hours
- Hours their manager approves
- Hours worked before noon

Q: Working "off the clock" is:
+ Not allowed; all work time must be recorded and paid
- Allowed if you volunteer
- Encouraged during busy weeks
- Required for training

Q: Time-clock rounding is lawful only if:
+ It is neutral over time and does not result in underpaying employees
- It always rounds down
- It always rounds in the employer's favor
- It rounds to the nearest hour

Q: Clocking in for a coworker is:
+ Time fraud
- Teamwork
- Allowed for friends
- Required on busy days

Q: Short rest breaks of about 5 to 20 minutes are:
+ Counted as hours worked under the FLSA
- Unpaid
- Not allowed
- Deducted from PTO

Q: A bona fide meal period of 30 minutes or more is unpaid only if:
+ The employee is completely relieved from duty
- The employee eats at their desk while working
- The employee answers calls
- The manager says so

Q: Required training attended by a non-exempt employee is generally:
+ Working time
- Unpaid
- Volunteer time
- Vacation

Q: A manager editing a timesheet must:
+ Have a valid reason and leave an audit trail, with the employee informed
- Do it silently
- Reduce hours to meet budget
- Change only overtime

Q: Answering work messages at home after hours, for a non-exempt employee, is:
+ Work time that should be recorded
- Free time
- Not allowed to record
- A hobby

Q: Time records must be kept:
+ For at least the periods the law requires, often several years
- For a week
- Until payday
- Never

Q: If your paycheck does not match your hours, you should:
+ Report it to your manager or Payroll right away
- Ignore it
- Adjust the next timesheet yourself
- Quit
