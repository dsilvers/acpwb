# Antitrust and competition law.

Q: Agreeing with a competitor to fix prices is:
+ Illegal under antitrust law and can be a crime
- Fine if prices are reasonable
- Allowed at trade association meetings
- Allowed if agreed over lunch

Q: An agreement between companies not to hire each other's employees ("no-poach") is:
+ An antitrust risk that the Department of Justice may prosecute
- A standard courtesy
- Required by HR policy
- Allowed if it is unwritten

Q: Sharing current salary data directly with competitors to set wages is:
+ An antitrust risk; wage-fixing can be prosecuted like price-fixing
- A benchmarking best practice
- Required by {AGENCY}
- Allowed if done by email

Q: At a trade association meeting, a competitor starts discussing next year's pricing. You should:
+ Object, leave the meeting, and ask that your departure be noted, then report it to Legal
- Take notes
- Share your own pricing to be polite
- Stay quiet and listen

Q: Dividing customers or territories with a competitor is:
+ Illegal market allocation
- An efficient sales strategy
- Allowed if both companies are small
- Allowed in {CITY}

Q: Using properly aggregated, anonymized, and older compensation survey data from a third party is:
+ Generally lower risk than exchanging current data directly with competitors
- Always illegal
- Identical to price-fixing
- Prohibited by the Code of Conduct

Q: Documents and emails about competitors should:
+ Avoid exaggerated language like "crush" or "dominate the market"
- Use strong language to motivate the team
- Be written in code
- Be sent only after midnight

Q: "Bid rigging" means:
+ Competitors coordinating who will win a bid or what they will bid
- Building a stage for {EVENT}
- Bidding on office supplies
- A sailing term

Q: Antitrust laws apply to:

- Only written contracts
- Only large companies
- Only during business hours

Q: A customer asks you to match a competitor's price that the competitor told you about directly. This is:
+ A sign of improper information exchange; consult Legal
- Normal negotiation
- Required
- Smart sales

Q: Abuse of a dominant market position can include:
+ Predatory pricing designed to drive out competitors
- Offering good service
- Lowering prices because costs fell
- Hiring good people

Q: Antitrust violations can lead to:
+ Large fines for companies and prison for individuals
- A warning letter only
