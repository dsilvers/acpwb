# Investor relations and Regulation FD.
Q: Regulation FD prohibits public companies from:
+ Selectively disclosing material nonpublic information to analysts or investors before the public
- Talking to investors at all
- Publishing press releases
- Holding annual meetings

Q: If an investor or analyst contacts you directly, you should:
+ Refer them to Investor Relations
- Share your view of next quarter's results
- Answer only easy questions
- Ask what they already know

Q: Material nonpublic information is information that:
+ A reasonable investor would consider important and has not been publicly released
- Is printed on company letterhead
- Appears on the public website
- Is about the cafeteria menu

Q: A "quiet period" before earnings means:
+ The company limits communications with investors and analysts
- The office is silent
- Employees cannot use phones
- No meetings are held

Q: Mentioning unreleased revenue figures at a conference dinner is:
+ A potential Regulation FD violation and prohibited
- Networking
- Allowed after dessert
- Fine if the guest promises not to tell

Q: Only authorized spokespeople may:
+ Speak with investors and analysts on behalf of ACPWB
- Read the annual report
- Own company stock
- Attend shareholder meetings

Q: If material information is accidentally disclosed to an analyst, the company must:
+ Make it public promptly, generally within 24 hours or before the next trading day opens
- Ask the analyst to forget it
- Disclose it at the next annual meeting
- Do nothing

Q: Posting comments about ACPWB's stock on investor message boards is:
+ Prohibited for employees under the communications and insider trading policies
- Encouraged for loyalty
- Fine with a fake username
- Required for executives

Q: Earnings guidance may be updated:
+ Only through approved public disclosure channels
- In private calls with favorite investors
- By any manager
- On social media by employees

Q: The annual report and proxy statement are prepared with input from:
+ Finance, Legal, and Investor Relations under disclosure controls
- Whoever volunteers
- Analysts
- The cafeteria staff

Q: An investor offers to buy you dinner to "learn more about the business." You should:
+ Decline and refer them to Investor Relations
- Accept and share what you know
- Accept and share only rumors
- Ask for stock tips in return
