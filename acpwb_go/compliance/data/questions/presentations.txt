# Presentations, slide decks, and screen sharing.

Q: A slide deck shared outside ACPWB should be:
+ Reviewed for confidential information, including speaker notes and hidden slides
- Sent as-is to save time
- Sent with all comments visible
- Sent with "Draft" in 72-point font to excuse errors

Q: Speaker notes in a presentation file are:
+ Included when you share the file, so review them before sending
- Automatically deleted when emailed
- Visible only on {WEEKDAY}s
- Encrypted by default

Q: A chart that starts the y-axis at a value other than zero to exaggerate a small change is:
+ Misleading
- A best practice
- Required for growth charts
- Required by {AGENCY}

Q: Data in a presentation should:
+ Cite its source and date
- Be rounded to look impressive
- Be estimated if the real numbers are inconvenient
- Use as many decimal places as possible

Q: Before sharing your screen to present, you should:
+ Close unrelated windows and turn on "do not disturb"
- Open your personal email
- Leave notifications on for transparency
- Open the {DOCUMENT} in another tab

Q: A presentation with 80 slides for a 15-minute meeting is:
+ Too long; focus on key messages and use an appendix
- Ideal
- Required for executives
- Too short

Q: Using a competitor's confidential slides found online in an ACPWB deck is:
+ Not allowed; it may violate their rights and our Code of Conduct
- Clever research
- Allowed if you change the colors
- Allowed if you cite them

Q: Accessibility for slides includes:
+ Sufficient color contrast, readable fonts, and alt text for images
- Neon text on bright backgrounds
- 8-point font to fit more content
- Animations on every bullet

Q: The ACPWB slide template:
+ Should be used for external presentations to keep branding consistent
- Is optional, including for customer pitches
- May be modified freely with clip art
- Is only for internal parties

Q: Rehearsing a presentation helps because:
+ It keeps you on time and catches errors
- It is required by {AGENCY}
- It makes slides load faster
- It replaces the need for slides

Q: "Let's take this offline" in a presentation usually means:
+ Continue the discussion after the meeting with the relevant people
- Disconnect from the internet

- Cancel the project
