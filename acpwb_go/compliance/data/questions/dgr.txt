# Dangerous goods by air. Correct answers follow 49 CFR Parts 171-180,
# the ICAO Technical Instructions, and IATA DGR in general terms.

Q: Before anyone at ACPWB prepares a shipment of dangerous goods for air transport, they must:
+ Complete hazmat training and follow the classification, packing, marking, and labeling rules
- Wrap the box in extra tape
- Write "FRAGILE" on all sides
- Ask the courier to keep it a secret

Q: Shipping undeclared dangerous goods by air is:
+ Illegal and can bring large civil and criminal penalties
- Allowed for small amounts
- Allowed for internal mail between offices
- Fine if the box is unlabeled

Q: Lithium-ion batteries shipped by themselves, not packed with or in equipment, are identified as:
+ UN3480
- UN0001
- Office supplies
- Class 12 Mystery Goods

Q: Lithium-ion batteries shipped by themselves (UN3480) as cargo on passenger aircraft are:
+ Forbidden; they may go only on cargo aircraft, under strict limits
- Allowed in unlimited quantities
- Allowed if they are new
- Allowed if they are wrapped in bubble wrap

Q: For passengers, power banks over 160 watt-hours are:
+ Forbidden on passenger aircraft
- Allowed in checked baggage
- Allowed if fully charged
- Allowed if they fit in a pocket

Q: For passengers, spare lithium-ion batteries between 100 and 160 watt-hours generally require:
+ Airline approval, with a limit of two spare batteries
- Nothing at all
- A note from the CEO
- Packing in checked baggage

- Packing slip with a smiley face

Q: Spray paint and other flammable aerosols in passenger baggage are:
+ Forbidden in carry-on and checked baggage

Q: The Class 9 hazard label on a package indicates:
+ Miscellaneous dangerous goods, such as lithium batteries or dry ice

Q: If you find a damaged, leaking, or smoking package labeled as dangerous goods in the mailroom, you should:
+ Keep people away, notify your supervisor and the carrier, and follow the emergency response information

Q: Before an ACPWB event, a vendor offers to fly fireworks, fuel canisters, and lithium batteries to the venue as "regular freight." The correct response is:
+ Refuse; these are dangerous goods that require proper declaration and handling

- Accept and remove the labels
- Ship them in employees' luggage instead
