# Elevators and stairwells.

Q: [You are trapped|You become stuck] in an elevator between floors. You should:
+ Press the alarm or emergency call button, stay calm, and wait for help
- Pry the doors open and climb out
- Jump to reset the elevator
- Use the escape hatch

Q: During a fire alarm, elevators should be:
+ Avoided; use the stairs
- Used, if they arrive quickly
- Used by anyone carrying a laptop
- Held for the floor warden

Q: [Stairwell doors|The fire doors in stairwell C] should be:
+ Kept closed; they keep smoke out of the stairwell
- Propped open for air circulation
- Locked from both sides
- Decorated for the holidays

Q: [The elevator doors|Elevator doors] begin to close on [a colleague|{NAME}] carrying boxes. You should:
+ Press the door-open button rather than reaching into the doors
- Put your hand between the doors
- Kick the doors
- Let them close; the next elevator will come

Q: [An elevator|The {FLOOR} floor elevator] is making grinding noises and stopping unevenly. You should:
+ Stop using it and report it to Facilities
- Continue using it until it stops working
- Ride it alone, to reduce weight
- Mention it in your next survey response

Q: If you are trapped in a stalled elevator, you should:
+ Press the alarm or call button and wait for help, without trying to climb out
- Pry the doors open
- Climb through the ceiling hatch
- Jump to restart it

Q: An elevator that stops between floors with the doors partly open should be:
+ Left alone until trained responders arrive
- Exited by jumping down
- Pushed by hand
- Used anyway

Q: The elevator inspection certificate should be:
+ Current, and an expired one reported to Facilities
- Ignored
- Removed
- Laminated

Q: Overloading an elevator beyond its posted capacity is:
+ Unsafe and not allowed
- Fine if everyone breathes in
- Required at rush hour
- Allowed for moves

Q: During a fire alarm, elevators typically:
+ Return to a designated floor and are taken out of normal service
- Speed up
- Stop working forever
- Play music
