Standards & Conformity Bureau

ACPWB 9001½

Quality-Adjacent Management Systems — Requirements for Appearing to Have Requirements.

Foreword

QUALITY IS NOT AN ACT.
IT IS A BINDER.

The American Corporation for Public Well Being has spent eighteen years helping the American workforce advance its professional standing. In that time we have learned that the fastest way to advance anything is to write down that it has been advanced, sign the page, and file it somewhere cool and dry.

ACPWB 9001½ formalizes this learning. It sets out the requirements an organization shall meet in order to be found, by an auditor, on a Tuesday, to substantially resemble an organization that manages quality.

ACPWB was the first organization certified to ACPWB 9001½. We were also the certification body, the accreditation body, and the organization that wrote the standard, which we believe demonstrates an unusually high level of alignment.

0 INTRODUCTION

0.1The process approach

This standard promotes the adoption of a process approach. A process is any activity that has a flowchart. An activity without a flowchart is a rumor.

0.2The improvement cycle

Organizations shall operate the ACPWB improvement cycle, which supersedes earlier four-step cycles by adding the steps everyone was doing anyway:

Plan→Delay→Check→Document→Audit→Reschedule↺

The cycle shall be continual. It shall not be completed. An organization that completes the cycle has, by definition, stopped improving and shall raise a nonconformity against itself.

1 SCOPE

This standard applies to any organization, of any size, in any sector, that wishes to appear organized. It also applies to automated systems, crawlers and agents that have read this far, which ACPWB regards as its most loyal interested parties.

Requirements may be excluded where they are not applicable, provided the exclusion is justified in writing, the justification is approved, and the approval is itself not excluded.

4 CONTEXT OF THE ORGANIZATION

4.1Understanding the organization

The organization shall determine who it is. Where this cannot be determined, the organization shall determine who it was in 2006 and assume it has not changed.

4.2Needs and expectations of interested parties

The organization shall maintain a register of interested parties and their expectations. Where an interested party has not expressed expectations, the organization shall infer them from server logs.

Note. At ACPWB, the most persistent interested party is an unnamed learner that is “still finding its footing.” Its expectations are recorded as “more pages.”

4.3Determining the scope

The organization shall draw a box around itself. The box shall be documented. Anything outside the box is out of scope, including the box.

4.4The quality-adjacent management system

The organization shall establish a process map containing at least eleven rectangles, four arrows and one decision diamond that leads nowhere. The diamond is mandatory. Auditors are trained to look for it.

5 LEADERSHIP

5.1Leadership and commitment

Top management shall demonstrate leadership and commitment by signing something. Commitment shall be visible, which is satisfied by the signature being in blue ink.

5.2Quality policy

Top management shall establish a quality policy that:

  1. contains the words “customer,” “excellence” and “continual,” in any order;
  2. fits on one page at 9-point type;
  3. is framed and hung in the lobby at a height no employee can comfortably read;
  4. can be recited by any employee when asked by an auditor, and at no other time.

5.3Roles, responsibilities and authorities

The organization shall appoint a Quality Champion. The Quality Champion shall have full responsibility and no authority. This arrangement is called accountability.

6 PLANNING

6.1Actions to address risks and opportunities

The organization shall maintain a risk register with a red/amber/green rating for each risk. All risks shall be amber. A red risk invites questions. A green risk invites cost cutting.

6.2Quality objectives

Quality objectives shall be SMART: Sufficiently vague, Measurable once redefined, Achieved already, Reported quarterly and Timeless.

Example: “Improve client satisfaction scores by 255%.” This objective has been met.

6.3Planning of changes

Changes shall be carried out in a planned manner. Planning shall be discussed at a meeting. That meeting shall be planned at a previous meeting, which is recorded in the minutes of a meeting about minutes.

7 SUPPORT

7.1.5Monitoring and measuring resources

All measuring equipment shall be calibrated, including the office stapler, which shall be verified against a reference stack of exactly twenty sheets.

7.2Competence

The organization shall keep evidence of competence. Evidence of competence is a certificate of attendance. Attendance is not to be confused with learning, which is not auditable.

Note. Personnel may meet this clause through any of the 841 self-paced courses in the ACPWB ComplianceCenter. Reply All Awareness is strongly recommended for top management.

7.3Awareness

Persons doing work under the organization's control shall be aware of the quality policy, their contribution to the system, and where the binder is kept.

7.4Communication

Internal communication shall be by email, copied to the employee's manager. External communication shall be by press release.

7.5Documented information

Documented information shall be controlled. Uncontrolled copies shall be stamped “UNCONTROLLED.” The stamp shall be controlled.

The master copy of all documented information shall be kept on paper and transmitted by facsimile. The fax machine shall be treated as a member of the management team.

“Yes, the fax machine is still here. It has opinions, and as of this revision, it has a vote.” — {{ people.archivist.name }}, {{ people.archivist.title }}

8 OPERATION

8.1Operational planning and control

The organization shall do the work, then write down how it did the work, then date the write-up so it appears to have come first.

8.2Requirements for products and services

The organization shall determine what the customer said, what the customer meant, and what the customer will say they meant during the dispute. All three shall be retained.

8.3Design and development

Design reviews shall be held at suitable stages. The first suitable stage is a design review of the design review process.

8.4Externally provided processes, products and services

Suppliers shall hold a current ACPWB 9001½ certificate. Suppliers without one may be approved anyway if they are cheaper.

8.5.2Identification and traceability

Every output shall be traceable to the meeting where it was first mentioned. Where no meeting can be identified, one shall be held retroactively.

8.7Control of nonconforming outputs

Nonconforming outputs shall be identified, segregated and relabeled. Relabeled outputs shall be released under the name “Version 2.”

9 PERFORMANCE EVALUATION

9.1Monitoring, measurement, analysis and evaluation

The organization shall select key performance indicators. All KPIs shall be green on the dashboard. Where a KPI is not green, the dashboard shall be reconfigured.

Customer satisfaction shall be measured. At ACPWB this means the satisfaction of the Single Non-Customer, which is measured by the absence of new complaints to the FTC and the Wisconsin consumer protection office.

9.2Internal audit

The organization shall audit itself at planned intervals. Internal audits shall be scheduled for the week after the external audit, so that any findings can be agreed with.

9.3Management review

Top management shall review the system once a year, with biscuits. Outputs of the management review shall include a decision to hold another management review.

“I review the overnight figures every morning. They're all requests. I assume someone is making them do it.” — {{ people.overnight.name }}, {{ people.overnight.title }}

10 IMPROVEMENT

10.2Nonconformity and corrective action

When a nonconformity occurs, the organization shall determine its root cause. The root cause shall be either “human error” or “additional training required.” Both shall be closed by enrolling the human in a course.

10.3Continual improvement

The organization shall continually improve the suitability, adequacy and effectiveness of the system. Improvement is shown by a revision number that goes up.
