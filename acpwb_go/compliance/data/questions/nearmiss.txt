# Near misses and incident reporting (OSHA and NSC guidance in general terms).
Q: A near miss is:
+ An event that could have caused injury or damage but did not
- An injury that almost needed first aid
- A missed deadline
- A coworker who almost arrived late

Q: Near misses should be reported because:
+ They reveal hazards before someone gets hurt
- They count against your team's bonus
- They are required to be hidden
- They are funny stories

Q: A box falls from a high shelf and lands where someone was standing a second earlier. You should:
+ Report it as a near miss and help get the storage fixed
- Laugh and move on
- Put the box back on the same shelf
- Wait until someone is hurt

Q: Reporting a near miss:
+ Should never lead to punishment for the reporter
- Is grounds for discipline
- Requires a witness
- Must be done anonymously

Q: Root cause analysis of a near miss asks:
+ Why the hazard existed and what will stop it from happening again
- Who should be blamed
- How to hide it
- Whether the report can be deleted

Q: Employees have the right to report injuries and hazards:
+ Without retaliation, under OSHA rules
- Only through their supervisor
- Only if their manager agrees
- Only once a year

Q: Incentive programs that discourage reporting injuries:
+ Can violate OSHA's anti-retaliation rules
- Are required
- Improve safety
- Are a best practice

Q: A near miss report should include:
+ What happened, where, when, and what could have happened
- Only the reporter's opinion of coworkers
- Nothing but a signature
- A list of suspects

Q: A slippery spot that nearly caused a fall should be:
+ Reported, marked, and cleaned up
- Avoided by you only
- Left for the next shift
- Covered with a rug

Q: Corrective actions from a near miss should be:
+ Assigned an owner and a due date and tracked to completion
- Discussed and forgotten
- Optional
- Delegated to visitors

Q: Leading indicators of safety, such as near miss reports, are valuable because:
+ They show risk before injuries happen
- They look good in slides only
- They replace training
- They are easy to fake
