# The Year 2000 problem and other date bugs, for the archive-minded.
Q: The Y2K problem came from:
+ Programs storing years as two digits, so 2000 could be read as 1900
- Computers that could not count past 1999 megabytes
- A virus written in 1999
- A shortage of calendars

Q: Y2K remediation involved:
+ Finding, fixing, and testing date logic in programs and data
- Turning off all computers on December 31
- Changing the year to 1999 permanently
- Buying new calendars

Q: "Windowing" was a Y2K fix that:
+ Interpreted two-digit years within a set range, such as 00–49 as 2000s
- Opened office windows
- Removed dates from programs
- Added a Windows license

Q: Was the year 2000 a leap year?
+ Yes; years divisible by 400 are leap years
- No; century years are never leap years
- Only in Europe
- Only on the mainframe

Q: The "Year 2038 problem" affects systems that:
+ Store time as a signed 32-bit count of seconds since January 1, 1970
- Use paper calendars
- Use COBOL only
- Were built after 2038

Q: A program that calculates age as 2025 minus a two-digit birth year of 85:
+ Gives the wrong answer unless the century is handled
- Works perfectly
- Crashes the building
- Calculates 40 correctly every time

Q: Old COBOL programs that still run payroll today should be:
+ Maintained and tested by people who understand them
- Left untested
- Replaced overnight without testing
- Ignored

Q: The lesson of Y2K for modern systems is:
+ Test edge cases such as dates, time zones, and leap years
- Dates never matter
- Only use two-digit years
- Avoid calendars

Q: Contingency plans for Y2K included:
+ Manual backup procedures and staff on site at midnight
- Leaving town
- Unplugging the internet
- Writing letters to the year 2000

Q: Daylight saving time changes can cause bugs when:
+ Code assumes every day has exactly 24 hours
- Clocks are digital
- People sleep in
- It is sunny

Q: Storing dates in a standard format such as ISO 8601 (YYYY-MM-DD) helps:
+ Avoid ambiguity between formats like 03/04 and 04/03
- Make dates longer for fun
- Hide dates
- Confuse auditors
