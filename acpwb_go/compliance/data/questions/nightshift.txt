# Night shifts, fatigue, and after-hours building rules.

Q: Fatigue from night shifts increases the risk of:
+ Errors and accidents, especially in safety-sensitive work
- Productivity
- Better decisions
- Nothing

Q: Strategies to manage night shift fatigue include:
+ Consistent sleep schedules, short breaks, and avoiding heavy meals late in the shift
- Energy drinks every hour
- Skipping sleep
- Working double shifts

Q: After-hours access to the building requires:
+ Your badge and following the after-hours procedures
- Propping the door
- A coworker's badge
- Calling a friend

Q: Driving home drowsy after a long night shift:
+ Is dangerous; consider a nap, a ride, or public transportation
- Is fine with loud music
- Is fine with the window open
- Is required

Q: Building alarms set after hours should be:
+ Respected; follow the procedures for arming and disarming
- Disabled for convenience
- Shared via the team chat
- Ignored

Q: Non-exempt employees working a night shift must:
+ Record all hours worked accurately
- Work off the clock
- Record only daytime hours
- Be paid in PTO

Q: Lighting in the parking lot that is out should be:
+ Reported to Facilities or Security
- Ignored
- Fixed by climbing the pole
- Reported in a year

Q: Consecutive long shifts without enough rest:
+ Increase fatigue risk and may be limited by policy
- Are required
- Improve performance
- Are encouraged
