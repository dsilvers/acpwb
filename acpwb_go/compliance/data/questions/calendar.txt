# Calendar invites: procedures and courtesy.

Q: [A meeting invitation|Every meeting invite] should include:
+ A purpose or agenda, the right attendees, and a meeting link or room
- Only a subject line, to save time
- The organizer's performance rating
- A list of who declined last time

Q: [You receive an invite|An invite arrives] for a meeting you cannot attend. Courtesy requires you to:
+ Decline promptly, ideally with a note or a suggested alternative time
- Accept it and not attend
- Ignore it until the meeting starts
- Forward it to someone else without telling the organizer

Q: [Scheduling a meeting|Booking a meeting] over a colleague's blocked "focus time" is:
+ Avoided unless it is urgent, and then only after asking them
- Allowed, since focus time is optional
- Required for meetings with executives
- Encouraged on {WEEKDAY}s

Q: [Forwarding a meeting invite|Adding new attendees to someone else's meeting] should be done:
+ With the organizer's agreement, especially for confidential topics
- Freely, since invites are public
- Only to external guests
- Without telling anyone

Q: [Optional attendees|People marked "optional"] on an invite are:
+ Welcome to attend but not expected to; the meeting should not depend on them
- Required to attend anyway
- Required to send a substitute
- Removed if they decline

Q: [A recurring meeting|A recurring weekly meeting] that has lost its purpose should be:
+ Reviewed by the organizer and shortened, made less frequent, or cancelled
- Kept indefinitely out of tradition
- Moved to {TIMEOFDAY} to discourage attendance
- Renamed and continued

Q: [When scheduling across time zones|For attendees in other time zones], the organizer should:
+ Check each attendee's working hours and use the calendar's time zone settings
- Schedule in Milwaukee time and let others adjust
- Avoid inviting anyone outside Central time
- Send the invite without a time

Q: [Ending meetings a few minutes early|Speedy meetings that end 5 to 10 minutes early] are encouraged so that:
+ Attendees have time to get to their next meeting or take a break
- The organizer can leave first
- Meetings can start late
- The room can be double-booked

Q: [A private medical appointment|Personal appointments] on your work calendar should be:
+ Marked private, so only the time shows as busy
- Labelled in full detail
- Kept off the calendar and explained afterward
- Shared with the whole team

Q: [Double-booking yourself|Accepting two meetings at the same time] is:
+ Best avoided; decline or reschedule one and tell the organizer
- A sign of importance
- Required for senior leaders
- Solved by joining both on mute

Q: [An AI notetaker|An automatic recording tool] added to a meeting invite should be:
+ Disclosed in the invite, approved by the organizer, and allowed only if all attendees are told
- Added secretly to capture candid remarks
- Required for every meeting
- Invited as a regular attendee with no explanation

Q: [Lunch hours|Blocked lunch hours] on a colleague's calendar should be:
+ Respected, unless there is no other time and they agree
- Treated as open time
- Booked over for working lunches without asking
- Removed by IT

Q: [A meeting invite from an unknown external sender|An unexpected calendar invite from outside ACPWB] containing a link should be:
+ Treated with caution; do not click the link, and report it if suspicious
- Accepted, so it appears on your calendar
- Forwarded to your team
- Accepted and then deleted
