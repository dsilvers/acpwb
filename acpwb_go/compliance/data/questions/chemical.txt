# Chemical safety in shops and labs. Answers follow OSHA Hazard
# Communication (1910.1200) and Laboratory Standard (1910.1450) basics.
Q: Every chemical container in the workplace, including a spray bottle you filled, must:
+ Be labeled with the product name and its hazards
- Be unlabeled to keep things tidy
- Have the purchaser's name only
- Be labeled only if it is red

Q: Safety Data Sheets (SDS) must be:
+ Readily accessible to employees on every shift
- Locked in the manager's desk
- Read only by the purchasing department
- Discarded when the chemical arrives

Q: When diluting a concentrated acid, you should:
+ Add the acid slowly to the water
- Add the water quickly to the acid
- Mix them in your mouth
- Shake them in a closed container

Q: Pipetting by mouth is:
+ Prohibited; always use a pipette bulb or pipetter
- Fine for water-based solutions
- Required for accuracy
- Allowed if you are careful

Q: A chemical fume hood works best when:
+ The sash is kept as low as practical and work is done at least 6 in inside
- The sash is fully raised at all times
- It is used to store boxes
- The fan is turned off to save energy

Q: Oxidizers should be stored:
+ Away from flammable and combustible materials
- Next to flammable solvents to save space
- On the top shelf above eye level
- In unlabeled containers

Q: If a chemical splashes in your eyes, you should:
+ Flush with the eyewash for at least 15 minutes and get medical help
- Rub your eyes and keep working
- Wait for it to stop burning
- Flush for 10 seconds

Q: Chemicals should not be stored:
+ Above eye level or on the floor in walkways
- In approved cabinets
- By compatibility group
- With their SDS available

Q: Food and drinks in a chemical lab are:
+ Prohibited in areas where chemicals are used or stored
- Fine if the lid is on
- Allowed in the chemical refrigerator
- Encouraged during long experiments

Q: An unknown, unlabeled chemical container is found in the shop. You should:
+ Leave it alone and report it to EHS for identification and disposal
- Sniff it to identify it
- Pour it down the drain
- Use it if it looks like water

Q: The "signal word" DANGER on a chemical label means:
+ The chemical has a more severe hazard than one labeled WARNING
- The chemical is harmless
- The label is decorative
- The chemical is out of date
