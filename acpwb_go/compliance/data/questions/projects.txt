# Project intake and governance.
Q: New projects should be submitted through:
+ The project intake process with a business case
- Hallway conversations
- Personal favors
- Nothing

Q: A project charter defines:
+ Scope, goals, sponsor, and success measures
- Lunch plans
- Nothing
- Office seats

Q: Scope creep means:
+ Uncontrolled additions to a project's scope
- Creeping in the office
- Faster delivery
- Smaller scope

Q: Project status reports should be:
+ Honest, including risks and delays
- Always green
- Optimistic regardless
- Skipped

Q: Changes to scope, budget, or schedule should go through:
+ Change control with the sponsor's approval
- No process
- Secret decisions
- The newest team member

Q: Risks should be:
+ Identified, assessed, and tracked with owners
- Ignored
- Hidden
- Blamed

Q: Project closure includes:
+ Lessons learned and handoff to operations
- Nothing
- Deleting files
- Leaving early

Q: "Watermelon" status reports are:
+ Green on the outside and red on the inside, hiding real problems
- Healthy snacks
- Accurate
- Required

Q: Project budgets should be:
+ Tracked against actual spending
- Ignored
- Spent quickly
- Hidden

Q: Stakeholders should be:
+ Identified and kept informed
- Ignored
- Surprised
- Excluded
