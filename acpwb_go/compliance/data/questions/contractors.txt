# Managing contractors and contingent workers on ACPWB sites.
Q: Contractors working at an ACPWB site must:
+ Complete site safety orientation and follow ACPWB safety rules
- Bring their own rules
- Skip orientation if they are only here a week
- Be invisible

Q: Contractor access to systems should be:
+ Limited to what the job needs and removed when the work ends
- The same as a full-time admin
- Left active in case they come back
- Shared through a team account

Q: Directing a contractor's daily work like an employee can create:
+ Worker misclassification risk
- Free overtime
- A faster promotion
- A new benefit plan

Q: Contractors should be paid:
+ Through the approved purchase order and invoice process
- In cash from petty cash
- By adding them to payroll
- With gift cards

Q: A contractor has an unsafe practice on the job. You should:
+ Stop the work if there is an immediate hazard, and report it to the contract owner and EHS
- Let it go; they are not our employees
- Post a video online
- Fix it yourself without telling anyone

Q: Before a contractor handles confidential data, ACPWB needs:
+ A signed agreement with confidentiality terms
- A handshake
- Nothing; contractors are trusted by default
- A quiz score

Q: Contractor badges should:
+ Look different from employee badges and expire when the contract ends
- Be identical to employee badges
- Never expire
- Be shared among the crew

Q: Contractors are covered by ACPWB's harassment policy:
+ Yes; it protects contractors and applies to their conduct at our sites
- No; they are not employees
- Only after 90 days
- Only if they ask

Q: When a contractor engagement ends, the sponsor must:
+ Confirm return of equipment and badges and removal of access
- Do nothing
- Keep the contractor's laptop
- Extend access just in case

Q: Contractor insurance certificates are reviewed to confirm:
+ The contractor carries the coverage the contract requires
- The contractor's car is insured
- The contractor is a good driver
- Nothing; they are filed unread

Q: A contractor tells you about a coworker's harassment. You should:
+ Take it seriously and report it to HR
- Tell them to call their own employer only
- Ignore it
- Share it in team chat
