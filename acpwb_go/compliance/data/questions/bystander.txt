# Bystander intervention. Correct answers follow common bystander training
# (the "5 Ds": Direct, Distract, Delegate, Delay, Document).
Q: The "5 Ds" of bystander intervention are:
+ Direct, Distract, Delegate, Delay, and Document
- Deny, Defend, Deflect, Delete, and Depart
- Donuts, Dessert, Dinner, Drinks, and Dancing
- Decide, Debate, Draft, Distribute, and Deploy

Q: "Distract" means:
+ Interrupting the situation indirectly, such as asking the target for help with something
- Telling everyone to look at their phones
- Leaving the building
- Starting a fire drill

Q: "Delegate" means:
+ Getting help from someone with more authority or ability, such as a manager or Security
- Assigning the problem to the newest employee
- Forwarding it to a vendor
- Asking the harasser to handle it

Q: "Delay" means:
+ Checking in with the person afterward and offering support
- Waiting until next year's survey
- Postponing the meeting
- Delaying your lunch break

Q: When documenting an incident you witnessed, you should:
+ Note what happened, when, and where, and give the record to the person targeted or to HR as they wish
- Post the video online immediately
- Share it in the team chat
- Edit it to make it more dramatic

Q: Before intervening directly, a bystander should consider:
+ Their own safety and the safety of the person being targeted
- Whether the target is a top performer
- How it will look in their own review
- Whether there is an audience

Q: The "bystander effect" refers to:
+ People being less likely to help when many others are present
- People standing in doorways
- Bystanders receiving overtime
- The effect of standing desks

Q: {NAME} sees a coworker cornered at the holiday party by someone making sexual comments. A good "distract" option is:
+ Walking over and asking the coworker to come help with something across the room
- Laughing along so things stay friendly
- Leaving the party
- Waiting to see if it gets worse

Q: If you are not comfortable intervening at the moment, you can still:
+ Report what you saw and check in with the person later
- Do nothing; only managers can help
- Spread the story to others
- Post an anonymous meme about it

Q: Intervening as a bystander is protected because:
+ Reporting or opposing harassment is protected from retaliation
- It earns bonus points in the review cycle
- It is required only of HR
- It counts as a training hour

Q: The most effective bystander response is usually:
+ The one you can do safely and that supports the person targeted
- The loudest possible confrontation
- Ignoring it
- Filming without saying anything
