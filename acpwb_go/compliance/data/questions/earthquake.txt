# Earthquakes and building emergencies. Correct answers follow FEMA and
# Ready.gov guidance; keep them accurate.

Q: When an earthquake starts, you should:
+ Drop, Cover, and Hold On
- Run outside immediately
- Stand in a doorway
- Take the elevator to the lobby

Q: The best place to take cover during an earthquake is:
+ Under a sturdy desk or table, away from windows and things that could fall
- Next to a tall bookcase
- Near the windows to see outside
- In the elevator

Q: If there is no table nearby during an earthquake, you should:
+ Drop to the ground next to an interior wall and cover your head and neck with your arms
- Run to the stairs
- Stand on a chair
- Hold onto a window frame

Q: After the shaking stops, you should:
+ Check yourself and others for injuries, expect aftershocks, and follow evacuation instructions
- Immediately use the elevator
- Light a candle to see
- Return to your desk and finish emails

Q: During a power outage in the office, you should:
+ Use a flashlight, not candles, and follow instructions from floor wardens
- Light candles for ambiance
- Use the elevator
- Unplug the server room

Q: If you smell gas after an earthquake, you should:
+ Leave the area and report it, without using light switches or open flames
- Light a match to find the leak
- Turn on the lights
- Open the gas valve wider

Q: Securing tall furniture and shelves to walls helps:
+ Prevent them from falling during an earthquake
- Make the office look tidy
- Hide cables
- Improve Wi-Fi

Q: During a flood warning, you should never:
+ Walk or drive through flood water; turn around, don't drown
- Move to higher ground
- Follow official instructions
- Monitor weather alerts
