# Retaliation. Answers follow EEOC and whistleblower guidance in general terms.
Q: Retaliation means:
+ Taking adverse action against someone because they engaged in protected activity
- Any criticism of an employee
- Giving honest performance feedback
- Changing team structures

Q: Protected activity includes:
+ Reporting harassment or discrimination, participating in an investigation, or requesting an accommodation
- Arriving late
- Refusing all assignments
- Posting confidential data

Q: Retaliation claims are:
+ The most frequently alleged basis in EEOC charges
- Rare
- Never investigated
- Only filed by managers

Q: An adverse action can include:
+ Firing, demotion, a pay cut, a bad schedule, or exclusion that would discourage a reasonable worker from complaining
- Only termination
- Only physical threats
- Nothing short of a lawsuit

Q: A manager learns an employee complained to HR about them. The manager should:
+ Not change the employee's treatment because of it, and let HR handle the complaint
- Reduce the employee's hours
- Confront the employee
- Ask coworkers to avoid them

Q: An employee whose complaint was not proven:
+ Is still protected from retaliation if they made it in good faith
- Can be fired for complaining
- Must apologize
- Loses all protections

Q: Retaliation can also be directed at:
+ Witnesses and people associated with the person who complained
- Only the person who complained
- No one
- Only customers

Q: "Since you went to HR, you're off the big account" is an example of:
+ Retaliation
- Performance management
- Workload balancing
- Mentoring

Q: A manager can still address genuine performance problems after a complaint if:
+ Decisions are based on documented, legitimate reasons consistent with how others are treated
- They act quickly before HR finds out
- They mention the complaint
- They never document anything

Q: If you believe you are experiencing retaliation, you should:
+ Report it to HR, Legal, or the Ethics Hotline
- Retaliate back
- Stay silent
- Quit immediately

Q: Telling coworkers to stop talking to someone who reported misconduct is:
+ Retaliation and prohibited
- Allowed for team cohesion
- Required during investigations
- A neutral act
