# Respectful workplace and civility.
Q: A respectful workplace means:
+ Treating coworkers with courtesy and dignity, even during disagreements
- Never disagreeing
- Agreeing with your manager on everything
- Only being polite to executives

Q: Yelling at a coworker in a meeting is:
+ Disrespectful conduct that can violate the code of conduct
- Leadership
- A sign of passion that is required
- Fine if the project is late

Q: Constructive feedback focuses on:
+ Specific behavior and its impact, not the person's character
- Personal insults
- Rumors
- Sarcasm

Q: Repeated belittling comments about a coworker's work in front of others can amount to:
+ Bullying
- Mentoring
- Team building
- Performance management

Q: Respectful disagreement in a meeting sounds like:
+ "I see it differently; here's my concern"
- "That's a stupid idea"
- Silence followed by complaints later
- Eye-rolling

Q: Gossip about a coworker's personal life:
+ Harms trust and can create a hostile environment
- Builds teamwork
- Is harmless
- Is a team sport

Q: Excluding a coworker from meetings they need to attend to do their job:
+ Can be a form of workplace mistreatment
- Is a time-saver
- Is always acceptable
- Is a promotion strategy

Q: When you made a mistake that affected a coworker, the respectful step is:
+ Acknowledge it and help fix it
- Blame someone else
- Hide it
- Wait to be caught

Q: Respect in virtual meetings includes:
+ Being on time, muting when not speaking, and not multitasking visibly
- Eating loudly on camera
- Interrupting often
- Joining late every time

Q: Disrespectful behavior should be reported when:
+ It is serious, repeated, or you are not comfortable addressing it directly
- Never
- Only if you are the target
- Only after a year

Q: Swearing at customers or coworkers is:
+ Unprofessional and against the code of conduct
- A sign of honesty
- Allowed on Fridays
- Required in sales
