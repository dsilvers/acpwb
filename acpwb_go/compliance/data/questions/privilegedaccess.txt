# Privileged access management.
Q: Privileged accounts include:
+ Administrator, root, and other accounts that can change systems or security settings
- Accounts of employees who sit near windows
- Guest Wi-Fi accounts
- Accounts used only for email

Q: Administrators should use their privileged account:
+ Only for administrative tasks, not for email or web browsing
- For everything
- Only on weekends
- To check social media

Q: Just-in-time access means:
+ Elevated rights are granted only when needed and expire automatically
- Access granted just before the auditor arrives
- Access granted forever
- Access granted at midnight

Q: Privileged passwords should be:
+ Stored in an approved vault and rotated
- Written in a notebook
- Shared in chat
- The same across systems

Q: Privileged sessions on critical systems may be:
+ Recorded and monitored, as disclosed in policy
- Hidden from all logs
- Deleted every minute
- Broadcast publicly

Q: Granting yourself admin rights without approval is:
+ A serious policy violation
- A productivity hack
- Fine for experienced staff
- Required for troubleshooting

Q: The number of people with domain administrator rights should be:
+ As small as possible
- Everyone in IT
- Everyone in the company
- Unknown

Q: Break-glass emergency accounts should be:
+ Tightly controlled, monitored, and used only in emergencies with review afterward
- Used daily
- Shared freely
- Left without passwords

Q: Privileged access reviews should happen:
+ Regularly, with unnecessary rights removed
- Never
- Only after a breach
- Once in a career

Q: Service accounts with admin rights should:
+ Have restricted logon rights and strong, managed credentials
- Use the default password
- Be used by people to log in interactively
- Never be reviewed

Q: An unusual privileged login at 3 a.m. from another country should be:
+ Investigated as a possible compromise
- Ignored
- Celebrated
- Deleted from the logs
