# Goals, OKRs, and planning.

Q: OKR stands for:
+ Objectives and Key Results
- Office Kitchen Rules
- Organizational Knowledge Repository
- Only Keep Receipts

Q: A good key result is:
+ Specific and measurable
- "Do our best"
- "Be awesome"
- "Attend {EVENT}"

Q: SMART goals are:
+ Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, and Time-bound
- Simple, Modest, Average, Random, and Tardy
- Set by the {COMMITTEE} alone
- Goals for smart devices

Q: Setting a goal that is guaranteed to be met so it scores 100% is called:
+ Sandbagging
- Strategic excellence
- Stretch planning
- Balancing

Q: OKR scores of 0.7 in many organizations are considered:
+ A good result for an ambitious goal
- A failure requiring a PIP
- Perfect
- A rounding error

Q: Goals should be revisited:
+ When priorities change, not only at year end
- Never; they are set in stone
- Only when you forget them
- Every hour

Q: Cascading goals means:
+ Aligning team goals with company objectives
- Copying your manager's goals word for word
- Moving goals down the stairs
- Delaying goals one quarter at a time

Q: A goal like "increase customer satisfaction" becomes measurable when:
+ It includes a metric and target, such as raising survey scores from 7.2 to 8.0 by Q4
- It is written in bold
- It is repeated
- It is shared at {EVENT}

Q: Individual goals that conflict with team goals should be:
+ Discussed and aligned
- Ignored
- Kept secret
- Prioritized over everything

Q: Mid-year goal check-ins help to:
+ Adjust priorities and catch issues early
- Lower ratings
- Fill calendars
- Replace annual reviews entirely
