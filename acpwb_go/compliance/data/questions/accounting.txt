# Ethics in accounting and financial reporting.
Q: Financial records must:
+ Accurately and completely reflect transactions
- Show what management hopes
- Be rounded in our favor
- Be updated when convenient

Q: Recording expenses in the wrong period to meet a budget is:
+ Improper accounting and not allowed
- Smart budgeting
- Required at year end
- A best practice

Q: "Cookie jar reserves" refer to:
+ Over-reserving in good periods to boost results later, which misstates earnings
- A break room snack fund
- A type of savings account
- A recipe budget

Q: Journal entries should have:
+ Supporting documentation and appropriate approval
- No support
- A nickname
- Only verbal approval

Q: If a manager pressures you to make an accounting entry you believe is wrong, you should:
+ Refuse and raise the concern with Finance leadership, Legal, or the Ethics Hotline
- Make the entry
- Quit without saying anything
- Make half the entry

Q: Off-the-books accounts or funds are:
+ Prohibited
- Allowed for emergencies
- Required for flexibility
- A perk

Q: Sarbanes-Oxley requires public company CEOs and CFOs to:
+ Certify the accuracy of financial reports
- Write all journal entries
- Avoid audits
- Approve all expenses

Q: Misleading the external auditors is:
+ Illegal and prohibited
- Normal negotiation
- Fine if small
- Encouraged

Q: Reconciliations should be:
+ Completed on schedule, reviewed, and with differences explained
- Forced to balance with plugs
- Skipped when busy
- Done once a decade

Q: Estimates such as reserves must be:
+ Based on reasonable, documented assumptions
- Set to hit targets
- Random
- Copied from competitors

Q: Concerns about accounting or auditing matters can be reported:
+ To the Audit Committee through the Ethics Hotline
- Only to your manager
- Nowhere
- Only after leaving the company
